# Canotto Pizza Party — 12 pizzas, 48 h, Witt Fermo

Oct 5, 2026 · @Manuel

12 pizzas from one 48-hour plan: start Day 1 at 17:00, bake Day 3 at 17:00. Two bowls of 6 balls are mixed side by side on the same timeline, using 75% Caputo Pizzeria + 25% Manitoba W 380, Dr. Oetker instant dry yeast, 66% hydration and 270 g balls.

## Party timeline (48 h)

Do every step to bowl A, then bowl B. The only hands-on time is about 1.5 hours on Day 1 and 1 hour on Day 3.

| Time | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| **Day 1, 16:30** | Weigh everything for both bowls (see Formula). Make the yeast solution: one 7 g sachet in a glass, water added to a total of 70 g, stirred. |
| **17:00** | Each bowl: 599 g water at 18–20 °C + 5 g yeast solution, then 720 g Caputo Pizzeria + 240 g Manitoba, whisked together dry first. Mix until no dry flour remains. Cover, rest 20 min. |
| **17:20** | Each bowl: sprinkle on 27 g salt, add the reserved 30 g water a splash at a time, squeeze it in. Knead about 10 min until smooth and elastic. |
| **17:40** | Rest 15 min, covered. |
| **17:55** | Fold set 1, both bowls. |
| **18:15** | Fold set 2. |
| **18:35** | Fold set 3. Shape each dough into a tight ball, into a lightly oiled lidded container (4–5 L), mark the level. |
| **18:45** | Both containers into the fridge. |
| **Day 2** | Leave it alone. Some growth (up to about 1.5×) is normal. |
| **Day 3, 11:00** | Bowl A out of the fridge. Cut 6 × 270 g, ball them, into an oiled dough box or containers, cover, leave at room temperature. |
| **11:30** | Bowl B: same. The 30-minute stagger matches the order you'll bake them. |
| **14:30** | Check: balls should be relaxing and growing. If they're already doubled, move them somewhere cooler. |
| **16:00** | Toppings ready, mozzarella cut and draining, semola and peel set up. |
| **16:15** | Light the Fermo; preheat 45 min. |
| **16:55** | Stone at 430–460 °C (infrared thermometer). |
| **17:00** | Bake pizzas 1–6 (bowl A). Allow about 3 min per pizza including stone recovery. |
| **\~17:20** | Bake pizzas 7–12 (bowl B). Last pizza out around 17:40. |

**Two batches?** Not two separate sessions: one timeline with two bowls. 3.2 kg of dough in a single bowl is hard to fold by hand and needs a 10 L container, so splitting into 6-ball bowls is easier. The real bottleneck is the oven, which takes 35–40 minutes for 12 pizzas.

## The formula

| Ingredient | Baker's % | Per bowl (6 balls) | Total (12 balls) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Caputo Pizzeria flour | 75% | 720 g | 1440 g |
| K Plus Manitoba W 380 | 25% | 240 g | 480 g |
| Water (includes the yeast solution) | 66% | 634 g | 1267 g |
| Fine sea salt | 2.8% | 27 g | 54 g |
| Dr. Oetker instant dry yeast | 0.05% | 0.5 g = 5 g solution | 1.0 g = 10 g solution |
| Dough |  | about 1620 g | about 3240 g |

**Flour:** Caputo Pizzeria (W 260–280) is rated by Caputo for 8–12 hour rises, so on its own it's near its limit at 48 h. Blending in 25% Manitoba W 380 lifts the mix to roughly W 300, which handles 48 h comfortably. Whisk the two flours together dry before mixing. Don't go above about 30% Manitoba: the dough gets tight and springy and is harder to open. Save the Nuvola for another time; on its own it's also a good canotto flour.

**Why 66%:** canotto doughs run 65–78% hydration. Manitoba absorbs more water than Caputo Pizzeria, so the blend takes 66% as easily as Pizzeria alone takes 65%. If it's too sticky, drop to 64% (1229 g water total).

**Why 270 g:** this is the canotto standard (260–270 g) and opens to a 28–30 cm pizza with a tall rim. 12 balls gives your 10 guests one each plus 2 spare.

## Yeast by schedule

Dr. Oetker dry yeast is instant yeast: consistent and shelf-stable, so a good choice. Fresh yeast works just as well when it's fresh; dry simply removes that variable. Longer schedules use less yeast. Amounts assume a 22 °C kitchen and a fridge at about 4 °C.

| Version | Instant dry % | Instant dry, 12 balls | Yeast solution, per bowl | Fresh yeast equivalent |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 48 h | 0.05% | 1.0 g | 5 g | 0.15% |
| 36 h | 0.067% | 1.3 g | 6.5 g | 0.20% |
| 24 h | 0.083% | 1.6 g | 8 g | 0.25% |

Dry yeast converts at 1 g dry = 3 g fresh, the ratio the AVPN uses.

**Weighing tiny amounts:** most kitchen scales can't weigh 0.5 g reliably, so make a solution.

1. Empty one 7 g sachet into a glass and add water until the total weighs 70 g. Stir until dissolved.
2. Every 1 g of this solution now carries 0.1 g yeast: weigh out the grams in the table for each bowl.
3. The 634 g water per bowl already includes the solution, so add 599 g plain water + 5 g solution + 30 g held back.
4. Use the solution within a few minutes and discard the rest.

**If your kitchen isn't 22 °C:** use about 20% less yeast at 25 °C or above, and about 20% more at 19 °C or below.

## Hand-mixing method (all versions)

This takes about 1.5 hours, mostly resting. Holding back a little water and using folds instead of long kneading makes 68% easy by hand.

1. **Water and yeast.** Pour all the water except 30 g into a large bowl, then stir in the yeast (or solution). Use water at about 22 °C; for the 48 h version, use cooler water (18–20 °C).
2. **Add the flour.** Tip in all the flour and mix with one hand or a spatula until no dry flour remains, about 2 minutes. Cover and rest 20 minutes. Gluten starts forming on its own during this rest.
3. **Add the salt.** Sprinkle the salt over the dough, then add the reserved 30 g water a splash at a time. Pinch and squeeze the dough through your fingers until the water is absorbed, about 3–4 minutes.
4. **Knead briefly.** Knead for about 10 minutes, in the bowl or on an unfloured bench, until the dough is smooth and elastic and springs back slowly when poked. Wet your hands to stop sticking; don't add flour.
5. **Fold.** Cover and rest 15 minutes. Then do 3 sets of stretch-and-folds, 20 minutes apart: grab one side, stretch it up, fold it over, turn the bowl a quarter, and repeat 4 times. After the last set, the dough should be smooth and hold a rounded shape.
6. **Into the container.** Shape a tight ball and place it in a lightly oiled, lidded container about 3 times its size. Mark the starting level with tape so you can see how much it grows.

The yeast goes into the water and the salt goes in only after the flour, so the two never sit together undiluted.

## All three schedules (12 balls)

All three follow the same pattern: mix, short room-temperature rest, cold bulk in the fridge, ball the dough, final rise at room temperature. Times are hours from the moment you start mixing.

| Step | 24 h | 36 h | 48 h |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Instant dry yeast, total | 1.6 g (8 g solution per bowl) | 1.3 g (6.5 g per bowl) | 1.0 g (5 g per bowl) |
| Mix and folds | 0–1.5 h | 0–1.5 h | 0–1.5 h |
| Rest at room temperature | until 3 h | until 2 h | straight to fridge |
| Cold bulk in fridge | 3–17 h (14 h) | 2–30 h (28 h) | 1.5–42 h (40.5 h) |
| Ball the dough | at 17 h | at 30 h | at 42 h |
| Final rise at room temperature | 17–24 h (7 h) | 30–36 h (6 h) | 42–48 h (6 h) |
| Preheat the Fermo | from 23.5 h | from 35.5 h | from 47.5 h |
| Bake | 24 h | 36 h | 48 h |
| Example: bake Saturday 19:00 | start Friday 19:00 | start Friday 07:00 | start Thursday 19:00 |

**Balling:** ball the dough straight from the fridge or within 30 minutes. Cold dough is firmer and much easier to handle. Turn it onto a lightly floured bench, cut 270 g pieces with a scraper, and tuck each piece under itself into a smooth, taut ball. Put the balls in lightly oiled, lidded containers or a covered dough box with space between them.

**The final rise matters most.** Cold balls need 1.5–2 hours just to warm up before they start rising. Judge readiness by the dough, not the clock: see "Reading the dough" below. If they aren't ready at the planned time, wait; 30–60 minutes extra is normal.

## Opening and baking on the Witt Fermo

Aim for a stone at 430–460 °C, not the full 500 °C. At 68% hydration the rim needs 60–90 seconds to set inside; at 500 °C it chars before that happens.

**Opening for a tall rim:**

1. Flip a ball into a bowl of semola rimacinata so both sides are coated, then set it on the bench.
2. Press with your fingertips from the centre outward, leaving a 2.5–3 cm rim untouched. You're pushing the gas into the rim, so never press the rim itself.
3. Flip and repeat once.
4. Drape it over the backs of your hands and rotate, letting gravity widen it to 28–30 cm.
5. Lay it on a lightly semola-dusted peel, shake off loose semola, and top quickly. Keep toppings light, and drain fior di latte (cut it an hour ahead and leave it in a sieve).

**Baking:**

1. Preheat at least 30 minutes. The Fermo has no thermometer, so an infrared thermometer pointed at the stone centre is worth buying.
2. Turn the flame down to low just before launching, so the dome doesn't scorch the rim before the base cooks.
3. Launch, then wait 25–30 seconds until the back rim puffs and spots.
4. Turn the pizza 180° once (the Fermo's U-burner is designed for a single turn) and bake another 25–40 seconds.
5. Turn the flame back up and let the stone recover 1–2 minutes before the next pizza. The fixed stone loses heat with each bake.

## Reading the dough and troubleshooting

Balls are ready when they have roughly doubled, look relaxed and slightly domed, and show small bubbles under the surface. A floured fingertip pressed in should leave a dent that springs back slowly and only partly.

| What you see | Likely cause | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dent springs back fast; ball is tight and small | Underproofed | Wait 30–60 min more; next time add 0.05% yeast |
| Dent stays; ball spreads flat and tears when opened | Overproofed | Bake it anyway; next time cut yeast by 0.05% or ball 1 h later |
| Ball snaps back when stretched | Too cold or gluten too tight | Rest it 20 more minutes at room temperature |
| Rim burnt, inside gummy | Stone or flame too hot | Stone at 430–450 °C; flame low at launch |
| Pale, soft base | Stone not hot enough | Longer preheat; wait between pizzas |
| Flat rim despite good proof | Rim pressed while opening | Leave the outer 2.5–3 cm untouched |
| Dough too sticky to handle | Hydration high for your flour | Drop to 64% (1229 g water total) |

Keep a note of yeast, times and kitchen temperature for each bake. After two or three bakes you'll know exactly how your fridge and kitchen behave.

## Conflicts resolved from the 20-recipe document

| Question | What the document said | What I went with, and why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AVPN amounts and timing | 50–55 g salt and 3 g yeast per litre; total time given as 8–24 h, 8–12 h and 6–8 h in different places | It quoted the older rules. The [2022 AVPN rules](https://www.pizzanapoletana.org/public/pdf/Disciplinare_AVPN_2022.pdf) allow 40–60 g salt, 0.1–3 g fresh yeast and 1.6–1.8 kg flour per litre of water, with 8–24 h total fermentation |
| Dry vs fresh yeast | 1:3 in one place, 1:2 in others | 1:3, the ratio the AVPN rules state |
| Can salt touch yeast? | Picetto calls the rule a myth | The AVPN says avoid direct contact for more than 5 minutes; this method keeps them apart, so it doesn't matter |
| Does canotto need 70%+? | High-hydration examples at 70–78% | No. The [canotto rules](https://www.scattidigusto.it/pizza-canotto-10-regole-pizza-contemporanea) give 65–78% hydration, 260–270 g balls and pizzas up to 31 cm. The rim comes from fermentation and gentle opening, [not hydration alone](https://pandough.app/en/blog/what-is-pizza-canotto) |
| Hydration vs burning | Wetter dough "chars faster" | Wetter dough needs a slightly cooler, longer bake so the crumb sets, hence 430–460 °C rather than 500 °C |
| Bulk or ball early? | Mixed, depending on recipe | Cold bulk, then ball 6–7 h before baking. This matches the long-ferment majority and lets you ball firm, cold dough |
| Flour strength | W values varied; some recipes unspecified | A W \~300 flour is safest for 48 h; 75% Caputo Pizzeria + 25% Manitoba W 380 gets you there (note below), consistent with the [Pizza Italian Academy](https://www.pizzaitalianacademy.it/detnews.asp?idnews=89) example of W 300, 65% and 48 h cold |

**Caputo Pizzeria for 48 h:** Caputo rates it for 8–12 hour rises, and some guides say use a stronger flour like Caputo Cuoco for 48 h. Experienced home bakers report it holds up to 48 h in the fridge. Blending in 25% Manitoba removes most of that doubt; the [Caputo product page](https://www.mulinocaputo.it/en/categoria-prodotto/farina-per-pizza/) and [Pizza Making Forum](https://www.pizzamaking.com/forum/index.php?topic=79472.0) cover both sides.

Oven figures (500 °C stone in about 18 minutes, no built-in thermometer, single rotation) come from [Expert Reviews](https://www.expertreviews.co.uk/home-garden/outdoor-kitchen/witt-etna-fermo-pizza-oven-review) and [Witt's launch notes](https://fox40.com/business/press-releases/ein-presswire/631733649/witt-launches-pizza-ovens-with-rotating-stone-and-booster-burner/).
